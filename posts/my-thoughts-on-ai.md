*For simplicity's sake, I use the term "AI" to refer to LLMs and other related technologies throughout this post. None of these things are actually AI. Also, I ascribe human-like characteristics to AI throughout this post. This is also for simplicity's sake. AI ain't people, it doesn't think or feel, it has no motivation, et cetera.*

I'm a former musician, occasional author, off-and-on-again artist, tabletop roleplaying game designer, and computer programmer. Early on, before the various ethical ramifications of AI use were widely publicized, I dabbled with AI for all of those things. Back then, of course, it was mostly crap. But it was fun.

Recently, through conversations with folks in the tech industry, an industry I'm trying to break into, I've been told under no uncertain terms that I *must* learn to use AI if I hope to get a job. So, begrudgingly but with an open mind, I've learned how to use it. But I've since abandoned it again, because...

It's still mostly crap. (And it's still unethical.)

Yes, even being proficient in the use of modern AI tooling (mostly as it relates to programming), I can confidently say that it's still mostly crap. It creates the illusion of competency while being completely incorrect at many of the most fundamental things—things that a novice would likely fail to notice, especially because the AI spews nonsense with all the confidence in the world.

I hear a lot of folks saying things like, "It's fine for [thing I don't know how to do or fully understand], but it's really bad at [thing I'm good at and fully understand]."

That's just the Gell-Mann amnesia effect, my dude. Or maybe Knoll's law. But either way, AI as it is today is worse than useless. It's dangerous. Not because it's going to take over the world or replace skilled labor or anything like that. It is completely incapable of doing those things. No, it's dangerous because it creates the illusion of being capable of doing those things. And people without experience in the given fields are the most likely to fall for its illusions: middle managers, CEOs, board members, stockholders—the people who decide who to hire and who to fire and where to allocate resources.

I hear a lot of folks saying things like, "It's just like any new tool that makes work more efficient, and if you don't use it you will be left behind—it's just like taking a photo instead of painting or using a compiled programming language instead of writing code in assembly."

First of all, people still paint and write code in assembly language. But also, it's not like other technological advancements of the past. It's unpredictable and non-deterministic. Typically with new technologies, you learn them, you master them, and then you can use them to get intentional and reliable outcomes. Not so with AI. The same prompt can return wildly different outcomes. That's not a tool. (I was going to say "that's just rolling dice," but even with dice you can create a reliable probability curve!)

I hear a lot of folks saying things like, "The genie is out of the bottle, you can't close Pandora's box, the technology is here to stay and it will only get better."

The *Big Short* guy would like a word. He's betting against AI. The price of using AI is already going up, but once the free money finally dries up and folks have to pay the real cost of AI, it'll be cheaper to hire a junior engineer. And you'll ultimately get better work out of them, to boot.

I hear a lot of folks saying things like, "It levels the playing field, it allows things to be created that never would have been otherwise, it enables people to do things they never thought possible."

You know what else does all those things? Practice, effort, learning, trial and error. And through those grueling hours of study, you don't only learn the how, but you also learn the why and the why not. And in the end, after all that hard work, you've done something you can be proud of. You've done something human.

I'm not even touching on brain drain, ethics, theft, environmental concerns, and any number of other problems with AI. The list goes on and on.

I guess all of that is to say I stand by the statements I made on my [AI](/ai/) page on [this here website](/):

> I am a human being, and everything on this website—from the code to the words to the few images there are—was, and continues to be, made by human hands (mostly my own). This was and is done both for ideological reasons, e.g., current generative AI models are built on capitalist theft, and for practical ones, e.g., current generative AI output is typically trash.
